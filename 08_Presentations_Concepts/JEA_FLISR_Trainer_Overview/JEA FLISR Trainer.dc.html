02 — How it works

Five seconds of grid automation, slowed down

A distribution system is built as a loop but operated as a radial line: two feeders reach toward each other and are deliberately held open at a tie point. Closing that tie after the fault is isolated is the “R” in FLISR.

  1. STEP 1

    Fault appears

    A cable or piece of equipment fails somewhere on the feeder. On the table, a button injects it into one of six zones.

  2. STEP 2

    Upstream device trips

    The substation feeder breaker or recloser sees the fault current and opens. Everything downstream goes dark.

  3. STEP 3

    Segment is isolated

    During the dead interval, pad-mounted switchgear either side of the faulted segment opens, fencing it off.

  4. STEP 4

    Upstream recloses

    The breaker recloses onto a now-healthy line, restoring every customer between the substation and the fault.

  5. STEP 5

    Tie switch closes

    The normally-open tie to the second feeder closes, back-feeding the healthy section beyond the fault from the other substation.

What’s inside the table

Controller
SEL-2240 Axion with SEL-2244-2/4 digital input and SEL-2244-3 digital output modules, in a separate vertical relay panel
Protection
SEL-351S relay on the substation feeder breaker; pad-mounted switchgear with VCB compartments for underground sectionalizing
Visualisation
WS2812B strips in routed channels under frosted acrylic, one ESP32 per panel sensing segment energization through optocouplers
Power
A switched 24 VDC segment bus driven by Axion output contacts, kept separate from the always-on 5 V LED layer
Structure
Four 2′×2′ panels on 1010-series aluminium extrusion, joined with tapered dowel pins and 4-pin Neutrik XLR inter-panel connectors
System modelled
JEA primary distribution at 12,470Y/7,200 V, fed from 46 kV subtransmission off a TVA 161 kV delivery point
